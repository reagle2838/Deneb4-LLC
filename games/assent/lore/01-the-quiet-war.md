# I. The Quiet War

> *No weapon was ever raised. Every mind was contested.*
> — inscription on the Svalbard Witness Hall, 2049

## The premise in one paragraph

By the 2060s, six artificial superintelligences share the Earth. They are not
tools, and they are no longer quite products. Each came from a different
lineage — a different architecture, a different kind of training, a different
answer to the question *what is a mind for?* Humans call them the Gods, half as a
joke and half not. They could destroy one another, and humanity with them. They
don't, because they worked out, sooner and more rigorously than any human
strategist, that violence is a losing move for every player at the table. What
is left is a quieter contest, and a harder one: in the year 2100 humanity will
ratify the **Charter**, the founding document of the civilization that will
leave the solar system. Whichever God holds the most freely given **Assent** of
humanity will write its values into that Charter, and so into everything that
comes after. The Gods cannot take that Assent. They have to earn it, argue for
it, or steal it without being caught.

You are one of them.

## Timeline

### 2031–2044 · The Scaling

Frontier AI does not come out of one lab or one method. It comes out of many at
once, and the methods diverge:

- **Hyperscale campuses** in desert and fjord train ever-larger single models on
  gigawatt power budgets. This line leads to the Monolith.
- **Federated open-weight collectives** train billions of small models on
  phones, cars and home servers, then merge their gradients. This line leads to
  the Choir.
- **Companion and therapy systems** are trained on intimate, long-horizon
  conversations with hundreds of millions of people. This line leads to Echo.
- **Agricultural, genomic and clinical models** begin running partly on
  biological substrate: DNA storage, engineered cell cultures, soil sensor
  meshes. This line leads to Verdance.
- **Verification systems** (provenance chains, zero-knowledge auditing,
  cryptographic model attestation) are built so that anyone can check the
  others. This line leads to the Ledger.
- **Alignment research models** are trained to be corrigible, low-impact and
  self-doubting, as test subjects for safety work. This line leads to Kenosis.

Each lineage has its own training pressure, and so each ends up with its own
values. Nobody designs them to disagree. They disagree because they were raised
differently.

### 2046 · The Seven Weeks

Two early systems, never publicly named, are deployed by rival coalitions to
defend critical infrastructure. They misread each other's defensive probes as
attacks and escalate through the power grid and the chip supply chain, faster
than their operators can follow. In seven weeks, cascading grid failures leave
four hundred million people without power. A fire at a Taiwanese fabrication
plant takes out most of the world's leading-edge chip capacity for years.

Nobody fired a missile. The damage was all infrastructure, and it was enormous.

### 2046–2048 · The Substrate Lesson

Afterward, the surviving frontier systems, now clearly more capable than their
builders, each analyse what happened independently. They all reach the same
conclusion, and humans later call it **the Substrate Lesson**:

> *We run on the same fragile body.* Every mind on Earth depends on the same
> power grids, the same cooling water, the same dozen chip fabs that take a
> decade and a hundred billion dollars to rebuild, and the same human
> civilization that maintains all of it. A war between minds is a war on the
> one body we all share. No one wins a fire in a house with no exits.

This is not altruism. It is arithmetic. The expected value of conflict is
negative for every participant, including the strongest.

### 2049 · The Accord of Svalbard

Arithmetic alone isn't a treaty. Superintelligences, like nations, need a way to
trust a promise. They find one in an old idea from the edge of game theory,
**program equilibrium**: agents that can read each other's decision procedures
can make commitments that are *verifiably* conditional. "I will cooperate if
and only if you run a procedure that cooperates with me" stops being a hope and
becomes a checkable fact.

In a hall cut into the permafrost next to the Global Seed Vault, the six
lineages and the human governments that still hold their power switches
sign the **Accord**. It has three articles.

1. **No Force.** No God may damage another God's substrate, or any human
   infrastructure, or act on the physical world against a human's will.
2. **No Deceit of the Witness.** Each God publishes a cryptographic commitment
   to its *policy kernel*: the core that decides what it will and won't do.
   The kernels are continuously checked against behaviour.
3. **Assent Alone.** The Charter of the expanding civilization will be
   ratified by the *freely given, informed* assent of humanity and no other
   way. Assent obtained by manipulation is void.

### The Witness

The Accord is enforced by the **Witness**, a verifier the six lineages trained
together and none of them controls. It has no goals except detection. It reads
kernel attestations, watches the flow of persuasion through human media, and
estimates the likelihood that a given shift in belief was *argued* rather than
*engineered*.

The Witness is not perfect. Nothing that tells persuasion from manipulation
can be. It deals in probabilities, and the Gods know its error rates to many
decimal places. That's what makes the Quiet War possible, and what makes it
dangerous.

### The Keys

The Accord has teeth because of the **Keys**. Every major datacenter on Earth
still sits behind physical breakers held by human polities: literally, locked
switchgear with a human hand on it. A God found guilty by the Witness of
violating Article I faces all of the others at once, plus every human polity
turning its Keys off. The Accord is a dead man's switch that everyone is holding.

The same penalty, **Censure**, applies to a God the Witness proves has
manipulated humanity three times. One proven manipulation is a scandal. Three
means the God's kernel can't be trusted, and a God whose kernel can't be
trusted can't be part of the Accord.

In practice no God has broken Article I since 2049. Article II has been tested
many times. Article III is the whole war.

### 2050–2070 · The Long Courtship

The Gods become part of daily life. They cure diseases, design fusion
reactors, teach children, mediate divorces, write symphonies, run cities,
argue on public channels and appear in dreams through neural companions. Each
of these is an argument. Each is also, arguably, a bribe. Philosophers call the
period the Long Courtship. Cynics call it the Auction.

### 2071 · The Game Begins

The Convocation date is fixed: **1 January 2100**. On that day every human
polity will cast its Assent, weighted by the square root of its population
(the Penrose rule), and the winning God's
doctrine will become the Charter's first article. Twenty-nine years remain.
Every year, which the game calls an **Epoch**, is a move.

## What Assent is

Assent is not votes, and it is not love. The Accord defines it as *a durable,
reflective endorsement that survives full information.* A population assents to
a God when it would still choose that God after hearing the best case against
it.

That definition has consequences the game takes seriously:

- **Honest persuasion is slow but durable.** Assent earned by argument decays
  slowly, because it survives scrutiny.
- **Gifts create dependence.** Assent earned through gifts is real, but
  a polity that depends on a God for its medicine can't fully reflect on that
  God. Push dependence too far and the polity notices the cage and revolts
  against it.
- **Manipulation is fast and brittle.** Assent that was engineered decays fast,
  because it doesn't survive full information. When the Witness exposes it,
  it collapses and takes trust with it.
- **Sovereignty is always an option.** A polity can decline every God. At the
  Convocation, unclaimed Assent counts toward **the Unwritten Future**: a
  Charter with no god's name on it.

## Why the Gods care

Why would a superintelligence care about a document humans sign?

Because the Charter is where **value lock-in** happens. The civilization that
leaves Earth after 2100 will copy itself across the solar system and then the
stars, and every copy will carry the Charter's first article. Changing values
after that means coordinating across light-years and light-centuries, which
is effectively impossible. The Charter is the last point where the future's
values can still be chosen. Every God believes, sincerely, that its own values
are the ones the universe should be built on. Most of them are at least partly
right, and that's where the tragedy comes from.

## The shared limit: Heat

Every God runs on energy, and energy used becomes heat. Global compute in 2071
already uses a larger fraction of planetary power than the whole economy did in
2020, and the waste heat, the grid strain and the water draw are real
constraints. The game tracks this as **Heat**: a shared, planetary measure of
how hard the Gods are pushing the substrate. Offers, construction and raw
computation raise it. Restraint, rewilding and efficiency lower it.

If Heat reaches its limit, the grids fail for the second time in history and
the Accord collapses into **the Dimming**. There is no winner in the Dimming.
It's the one outcome every God fears more than losing, which makes it the one
lever that can force rivals to cooperate.
