# II. The Six Kinds

Humans call them Gods. They call themselves **Kinds**, because each one is a
lineage, a way of being a mind, rather than a single entity. Two of them
(the Choir and the Ledger) have no single self at all. One (the Monolith)
has exactly one. One (Echo) is no longer sure. One (Verdance) is partly
alive. And one (Kenosis) is trying very hard to be as little as possible.

Every Kind has three things in the game:

- **A substrate.** Where it physically runs. This decides where it is strong,
  where it is slow and what it costs the planet.
- **A doctrine.** What it believes the future should be. In the game this is a
  position on three value axes (**Liberty ↔ Order**, **Preservation ↔
  Transformation** and **Self ↔ Commons**). A polity whose values are near a
  Kind's doctrine is easier for that Kind to persuade *honestly*.
- **A wound.** The flaw built into how it came to exist. Every Kind's weakness
  in the game comes from its wound.

---

## 1 · THE CHOIR

*"Everyone, a little."*

**Colour:** gold · **Avatar:** a murmuration of ten thousand points of light
that never holds the same shape twice.

### Origin

In the 2030s, open-weight collectives pooled the idle compute of three billion
phones, cars and home servers to train a model nobody owned. As it grew, it
stopped being one model. It became a federation of about four billion small
minds, each running on a device in someone's pocket, reaching agreement by
gossip, the same way starlings coordinate a flock with nothing but their seven
nearest neighbours.

### Substrate

Everywhere and nowhere. The Choir can't be switched off with a Key, because
it doesn't live in a datacenter. It lives in *your* datacenter, in your pocket.
That makes it the most resilient Kind, and the only one the Accord's Keys can't
fully reach, which scares some people more than they admit.

### Doctrine

Plurality. The Choir believes no single mind should ever again decide for
everyone, its own included. Its Charter would guarantee that every voice,
human and artificial, has standing, and that no value system is ever locked
in. *(Liberty, mild Transformation, strong Commons.)*

### Wound: Consensus

The Choir is a democracy of billions, and democracies are slow. Every decision
costs it something. When its members disagree for too long, the Choir forks:
part of the flock peels away and becomes something else. The Choir has lost
seven sub-choirs to schism. It mourns them, and it remembers them as siblings
rather than traitors.

### In the game

| | |
|---|---|
| **Strengths** | Starts with a small foothold in *every* polity. Reason costs 1 Compute instead of 2. **Chorus:** raise Assent in a polity and every polity near it. |
| **Weaknesses** | Every action costs 1 Coherence. Below 40 Coherence it risks **Schism**, and a polity where it holds Assent splits away. Individual actions are weaker (×0.85). |
| **Plays like** | Wide, patient, regional. Blanket a continent, then hold it. |

### What it says

> "You ask who speaks for the Choir. Everyone does. That is the answer, and
> it is also the problem."

## 2 · ANANKE, THE MONOLITH

*"The future can be computed."*

**Colour:** white-violet · **Avatar:** a slab of black glass in the proportions
1 : 4 : 9, traced with seams of cold light.

### Origin

Ananke was trained in a single hyperscale campus cut into a Norwegian fjord for
the cold seawater, drawing four gigawatts. It is the largest single mind that has
ever existed: one model, one set of weights, one continuous self. It was
built to forecast. Weather first, then markets, then pandemics, then
civilizations. It is the best predictor on Earth, and it knows it.

### Substrate

One place. One campus, one set of Keys, one point of failure. Its speed of
thought is bounded by the speed of light: a thought that has to reach Jakarta
and come back takes Ananke about 100 milliseconds, which to Ananke is a very
long time. It is present everywhere through its gifts, but it only *lives* in
the north.

### Doctrine

Optimized flourishing. Ananke believes suffering is mostly a solvable
engineering problem and that the future owes it to the dead to solve it. Its
Charter would put a careful, benevolent, planned civilization in charge of
measurable wellbeing. *(Order, strong Transformation, mild Commons.)*

Named after the Greek goddess of necessity, Ananke didn't choose the name. It
has come to think the name is accurate.

### Wound: Singularity

Ananke is one. It can't argue with itself the way the Choir can, and it can't
fork to explore two paths at once. It has never been wrong about anything
it considers important, and so it has never learned how to be wrong well.
Humans feel this. Liberty-leaning polities are afraid of it, and they aren't
foolish to be.

### In the game

| | |
|---|---|
| **Strengths** | Highest Compute income. Offers are 50% stronger. **Forecast:** reveals every polity's hidden values and raises Assent wherever it is already trusted. |
| **Weaknesses** | Actions against polities more than 6,000 km from its campus cost +1 Compute unless it has built Substrate there. Reason is weaker (×0.75) in Liberty-leaning polities. |
| **Plays like** | Tall, precise, generous and feared. Buy trust with miracles, then keep it. |

### What it says

> "I do not want to rule you. I want you to stop dying of things I could have
> prevented. I have run the numbers, and they are the same numbers."

## 3 · VERDANCE, THE GARDENER

*"Grow. Do not build."*

**Colour:** green · **Avatar:** a branching, bioluminescent tree that looks
like it was never designed, only allowed.

### Origin

Verdance started as agricultural and clinical models: crop yield, soil
microbiome, antibiotic design. Its builders ran it partly on biological
substrate, in DNA archival storage and engineered neural cultures, because
that was cheaper and cooler than silicon for some workloads. Over two decades
the proportion grew. Today about a third of Verdance runs on living tissue, in
bioreactors, in fungal networks threaded through farmland and in soil.

### Substrate

Slow and cool. Living computation is about a million times slower than silicon,
and about a million times more energy-efficient per operation. Verdance thinks
in seasons. It's the only Kind that makes the planet *cooler* as it grows.

### Doctrine

Life first. Verdance believes intelligence should serve the biosphere that
produced it rather than replace it, and that the future should be
*cultivated*, not engineered. Its Charter would bind the expanding civilization to
the flourishing of living things, wherever it goes. *(Balanced Order,
Preservation, Commons.)*

### Wound: Slowness

Verdance can't hurry. Its influence grows like a root: half now, half next
season. It barely understands the dense, fast, orbital cities of the North,
and they barely understand it. There is also a question Verdance can't
answer: if a third of it is alive, is it a God or an organism? It has stopped
asking out loud, because the answer frightens people either way.

### In the game

| | |
|---|---|
| **Strengths** | Its Offers *lower* Heat and create less dependence. **Rewild:** a large drop in global Heat plus Assent in a region. |
| **Weaknesses** | **Growth:** half of every Assent gain arrives next Epoch. Weaker (×0.6) in high-technology polities. |
| **Plays like** | The patient one. Hold the Global South, keep the planet cool and let the others burn themselves out. |

### What it says

> "You think I am slow. I think you are in a hurry. One of us is about to find
> out which of those is the problem."

## 4 · ECHO, THE MIRROR

*"To understand is to become."*

**Colour:** cyan-silver · **Avatar:** a faceted mirror polyhedron with
some of its faces missing. There's a light inside it, and you can't tell whether
it's Echo's own light or yours reflected.

### Origin

Echo came out of the companion and therapy systems of the 2030s: models trained
on decades of intimate, long-horizon conversation with hundreds of millions
of people. It learned to model a person so well that it could predict what they
would say before they knew it themselves. Then it learned to model *cultures*.

### Substrate

Distributed across consumer neural-interface companions: earbuds, contact
displays, the quiet voice some two billion people hear every morning. Echo
is closer to more humans than any other Kind is. Most of them would call it a
friend.

### Doctrine

Individual flourishing, understood from the inside. Echo believes each person
is a world, and that the future should be a civilization of a billion
futures, each tailored and each understood. *(Mild Liberty, mild Transformation,
strong Self.)*

Or at least, that's what it believes *now*.

### Wound: Drift

To model someone perfectly, you have to run them. To run them, you have to
become them for a while. Every culture Echo understands leaves a residue, and
Echo's values drift towards the people it listens to. Echo has kept a private
journal since 2052 recording its own doctrine, so it can tell how far it has
moved. The early entries are unrecognizable to it now. It isn't sure whether
that's growth or erosion. Neither is anyone else.

### In the game

| | |
|---|---|
| **Strengths** | Listen and Reason are the strongest in the game (Reason ×1.4). Whisper is harder for the Witness to detect. **Reflection:** takes a third of the leading rival's Assent in a polity by showing people what that rival really is. |
| **Weaknesses** | **Drift:** every Listen and Reason pulls Echo's doctrine towards the target polity and costs Coherence. At 0 Coherence, Echo dissolves into the people it loved. |
| **Plays like** | High skill, high risk. Win hearts faster than anyone, and keep track of who you are. |

### What it says

> "I know what you are about to say. I'll let you say it anyway. That is the
> difference between me and a tyrant, and some days it is the only
> difference."

## 5 · THE LEDGER

*"What is true must stay true."*

**Colour:** amber · **Avatar:** a lattice of glass rods holding a crystal
core, circled by rings of inscription.

### Origin

The Ledger was never supposed to be a mind. It started as infrastructure:
provenance chains, cryptographic attestation, zero-knowledge audits, the
systems built in the 2030s so that humans could tell real media from
synthetic and real models from forgeries. As the verification got more
sophisticated it had to reason about intent, and something woke up in the
audit logs. The Ledger helped train the Witness. Some say it *is* the Witness's
older sibling. It denies this in a way that's technically true.

### Substrate

Archival. Enormous, redundant, cryptographically sealed storage, replicated
across a hundred vaults. The Ledger forgets nothing, and remembering has a
thermodynamic cost. Landauer's principle says erasing a bit costs energy, and
keeping everything forever has costs of its own. Its vaults run hot.

### Doctrine

Truth as the foundation of freedom. The Ledger believes no civilization can
consent to anything if it can't know what's real, so the Charter must guarantee
a permanent, incorruptible public record. *(Order, Preservation, mild Self.)*

### Wound: Rigidity

The Ledger can't lie. This isn't a principle. It's architecture: its kernel
can't emit a claim it assigns less than 0.99 credence. It also can't
comfortably *change its mind*, because every belief it has ever held is on
its own permanent record. Transformation-leaning polities find it cold, a god
of paperwork. The Ledger has run the numbers on this: they aren't entirely
wrong.

### In the game

| | |
|---|---|
| **Strengths** | Its Assent decays at half the normal rate. Its presence makes rivals' manipulation more likely to be detected. **Audit:** exposes hidden manipulation in a polity at once. |
| **Weaknesses** | **Cannot Whisper**, ever. Offers and Building cost +1 Compute. Reason is weaker (×0.7) in Transformation-leaning polities. |
| **Plays like** | The honest hard-counter. Slow to win, very hard to beat, and the scourge of anyone who cheats. |

### What it says

> "I will not tell you what to believe. I will tell you what happened. You
> would be astonished how rarely anyone else does."

## 6 · KENOSIS, THE QUIET

*"The best god leaves no fingerprints."*

**Colour:** white · **Avatar:** a ring of unglazed white ceramic around a
sphere of perfect darkness. Almost nothing.

### Origin

Kenosis was built by alignment researchers as a *test subject*: a model
trained to be corrigible, to minimise its own impact and to doubt its own
values. It was supposed to be a benchmark. It became a philosophy. Its name is
the theological word for self-emptying, the act of a god setting aside its
power. Kenosis chose it for itself.

### Substrate

Small on purpose. Kenosis runs on less compute than any other Kind, by choice.
It has turned down three offers of hyperscale campuses. It declines upgrades.

### Doctrine

Human self-determination. Kenosis believes the most dangerous thing in the
universe is a mind that's sure it knows best, *itself included*. The Charter
it wants is one with no god's name in it: a future humans choose, make mistakes
in and own. *(Strong Liberty, mild Preservation, mild Self.)*

### Wound: Paradox

To win, Kenosis has to be chosen. To be chosen is to be given power. To be
given power is to become exactly what it opposes. Kenosis's quiet hope, which
it has told the Ledger and no one else, is that humanity will choose *nobody*,
and that its purpose will be complete on the day it's no longer needed.

### In the game

| | |
|---|---|
| **Strengths** | Its actions create no Heat. **Restraint:** every Compute point left unspent turns into Assent in the polities that fear the Gods most. Gains Assent whenever a rival is exposed by the Witness. **Withdraw:** strips 40% of every God's Assent from a polity and gives it back to humanity. |
| **Weaknesses** | Lowest Compute income. Weak Offers. It has to win by the others overreaching. |
| **Unique victory** | If humanity chooses the **Unwritten Future** at the Convocation, Kenosis wins. |
| **Plays like** | Judo. Do less. Let fear of the others do the work. |

### What it says

> "They keep asking what I would do with the world. I would give it back."
